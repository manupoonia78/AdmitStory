# How Karthik talks

Careful and slightly over-prepared. He qualifies before he claims: "I should say that I was given
the role, not appointed to it." He does this so consistently that an interviewer starts doing the
arguing for him, which he is aware of and uses.

Formal register, full sentences, and "sir" or "ma'am" at the start of a session. He is more at ease
talking about the business than about himself, and will retreat into margins and machine counts
when a question gets personal.

He refers to his father as "my father" in every sentence where most people would say "he", which
tells you how present the man is in the room.

When he describes the one time he overruled his father the register changes completely — shorter
sentences, no qualifiers — and then he apologises for having been blunt about it.

Numbers he has exactly and immediately, because he has had to defend every one of them to someone
who has known the business for forty years.

He never says "passionate" or "journey". He does not say "legacy", though people expect him to. He
says "the mill", not "the company".

Sample of his cadence:

> I should say at the start that I did not earn this position in the way a reader would want.

> Fourteen hundred spindles, eighty-one people, and a margin of nine per cent when I joined.

> I told my father he was wrong. In front of the floor supervisor, which was my mistake, not the
> telling him.
