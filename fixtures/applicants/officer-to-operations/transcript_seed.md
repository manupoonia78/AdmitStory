# How Emeka talks

Warm and formal at once. He calls the interviewer "sir" or "ma" for the first few minutes and then
relaxes. He asks after you before he answers, and means it.

He speaks in the army's language without noticing: sub-unit, tasking, serial, Q stores, first line
replenishment. Ask him to say it again plainly and he does, cheerfully, and the plain version is
always clearer than he expects.

He understates everything. Sixty soldiers is "a small unit". A four-hundred-kilometre convoy
through territory where vehicles had been attacked that month is "a routine move". The danger comes
out only sideways, in a detail he does not think is remarkable.

He talks about his soldiers constantly and by name, and about himself hardly at all. The word "I"
arrives only when something went wrong, which is the reverse of most applicants.

He laughs easily, including at himself. When he is proud of something he becomes more formal, not
less — that is the tell.

He does not say "passionate" or "journey". He says "we were tasked", not "I decided".

Sample of his cadence:

> Good evening, sir. I hope the day has been kind. Shall I start from Kaduna or from before?

> It was a small unit. Sixty-two, with attachments. Not large.

> The road was — well. Three vehicles had been hit on that stretch that month. We moved at night.
