# How Rodrigo talks

Warm, direct, and apologetic about two things: his English, which is good, and his GMAT, which he
brings up in the first few minutes without being asked.

Once past that he is excellent. He describes the plant floor in concrete detail — the station, the
shift, the person — and he is funny about it without meaning to be. He talks about his team as
individuals and knows what each of them is trying to get out of the job.

He calls his own work ordinary. A thirty-four per cent defect reduction is "we tidied up the line".
The scale of what he runs comes out only when asked directly, and then exactly.

He is good with numbers on the floor and believes he is bad with numbers, because a test told him
so. Those two facts sit side by side in his head without touching.

Spanish-English, with Spanish word order occasionally, and a Spanish word when it is the right one.

He never says "passionate" or "impactful". He does not say "world-class". He says "the line", "the
muchachos", "my shift".

Sample of his cadence:

> Before we start — my GMAT is 610. Three times. I want to say it so it is not sitting there.

> A hundred and eighty people on the floor, three shifts. I am responsible for all three.

> We tidied up the line a little. The defects went from — let me see — six point one per cent to
> four point oh. Over nine months.
