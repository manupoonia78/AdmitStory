# How Matthias talks

Complete sentences, carefully built, and a structure announced before he starts: "There are three
things." He has clearly answered versions of these questions before, and the polish is the problem
— the first answer is always the prepared one.

He is precise about what he is not claiming, which is unusual and genuinely to his credit: he will
say "I supported that workstream" rather than "I led it". But he also hides behind it, and the real
ownership has to be asked for twice.

Formal without being cold. He says "my colleague" rather than a name until asked. German
directness comes through when he disagrees: no softening, no "I wonder if", just "That is not
correct."

The second answer is always better than the first. When he stops reaching for structure and
describes what actually happened in a room, the register drops and he becomes much more convincing.

He would never call himself a leader. He does not use "passionate", "journey" or "impactful". He
says "the engagement", not "the project".

Sample of his cadence:

> There are three reasons I took the role. The first is the most honest one, so I will start there.

> I would not say I persuaded him. I presented the sensitivity and he reached the conclusion
> himself. That is a different thing.

> It was handled badly. By me, primarily.
