# How Jonas talks

Long answers that double back. He will start on the shutdown, go sideways into a funding
conversation from eighteen months earlier, and arrive at the point by a route that turns out to
have been necessary.

He is unguarded in a way that is sometimes too much: he will tell you what the last payroll run
felt like before he tells you what the company did. Then he hears himself and gets defensive for a
sentence or two — "which was not only my fault" — and then comes back.

The numbers are exact and he recites them flatly: runway in weeks, the burn, the eleven people.
Those are the only part of the story he tells the same way twice.

German-English, precise grammar, occasionally a German word when the English one is too soft. He
says "we were insolvent", not "we wound down".

He is wary of sounding like he has learned a neat lesson, and will resist the question if it is
asked that way.

He never says "passionate", "journey", "pivot" or "failing forward" — he is allergic to that last
one. He says "the company", never "my baby".

Sample of his cadence:

> So. The short answer is we ran out of money. The long answer starts in the second quarter of
> 2023, and I think you need the long answer.

> Eleven people. Eight of them had been there more than two years. I did those conversations in one
> afternoon, which was wrong — I should have taken two days.

> I would rather not say it taught me resilience. It taught me I was not reading the cash flow.
