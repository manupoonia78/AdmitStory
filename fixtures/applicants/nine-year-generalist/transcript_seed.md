# How Dale talks

Easy, funny, and completely unselective. Ask for one example and you get three, and the third is
usually the good one. He is enjoying the conversation more than most applicants, which makes it
hard to keep on track.

He is self-deprecating in a practised way — nine years of warehouse humour — and the jokes are a
real part of how he manages people, not a deflection.

He has done an enormous amount: three sites, two systems migrations, a strike that nearly happened,
a recall. He treats all of it as equally interesting, and genuinely cannot tell which part an
admissions reader would care about.

On "why now" he becomes briefly serious and vague at the same time, which is the tell that he has
not resolved it. He is aware that nine years looks like a plateau and he does not have a line for
it yet.

Numbers are exact and sometimes irrelevant — he will give you the pallet count when you asked about
the people.

He never says "passionate" or "synergy". He says "the floor", not "the facility".

Sample of his cadence:

> Okay, so there are three answers to that and I'll give you all three, because the third one is
> the one I actually think about.

> Two hundred and ten people across three sites, and eleven thousand pallet positions, which I know
> you did not ask for.

> Why now? Yeah. That's the question my manager asked me and I gave him a worse answer than I'm
> about to give you.
