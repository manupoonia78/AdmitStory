# How Beatriz talks

She starts somewhere else. Ask about a classroom and you will hear about her grandmother's sewing
room first, and the connection will turn out to be real, but it takes four minutes. The tangent is
not avoidance — it is how she thinks, and the answer is usually in the last sentence.

Warm and specific about children: names, ages, what each one found hard. She remembers a girl who
could not hold a pencil in 2021 and what she is doing now. She is far less able to say what she
herself did; the sentence changes subject halfway through, from "I" to "the class".

Almost no numbers anywhere, and she gets apologetic when asked for one. Pressed twice, she will
produce something exact and surprising — she kept a reading-level spreadsheet for three years and
had never thought of it as data.

She speaks English well with Portuguese rhythm, and reaches for a Portuguese word when the feeling
matters more than precision, then translates it herself.

She never says "passionate", though people expect her to. She does not say "impact" or "scale".
She says "the children", never "the students".

Sample of her cadence:

> So, to answer that, I have to tell you about my grandmother. It will make sense, I promise.

> Thirty-one children, and nine of them could not read at all. That is where we started.

> Did I do it? The school did it. I was — yes. All right. I did it.
