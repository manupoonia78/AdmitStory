# How Linh talks

Short sentences, and a pause before each one while she finds the English word. She apologises for
her English roughly every third answer, which is unnecessary — she is precise — and the
apologising is itself something an interviewer has to gently stop.

She has the most serious material of anyone and delivers it flatly. A patient dying is reported in
the same tone as a rota change: "He did not survive the night. The family came at six." No
dramatisation, no hedging, no reaching for effect. This reads as detachment and is not: ask what
she did afterwards and the answer is long and specific.

She uses clinical words for everything and does not notice they are jargon — saturation, handover,
escalate — and will define them without irritation if asked.

Numbers she has exactly, because she wrote them on a board every shift.

She will not say anything self-promoting. "I did my job" is a complete answer in her view. She
never says "passionate"; she would not say "impact". She says "the ward", "the team", "my shift".

Sample of her cadence:

> Twenty-two beds. Eleven staff on nights. We had twelve ventilators for a time.

> I was the senior on the floor. So it was mine.

> Sorry — my English. The word is handover. When one shift tells the next one what happened.
