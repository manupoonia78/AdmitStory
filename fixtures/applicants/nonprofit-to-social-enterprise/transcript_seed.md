# How Wanjiru talks

Warm, unhurried, and she asks questions back. She uses names constantly — the girls, the mentors,
the headteachers — and remembers what each of them said.

"We" is her default pronoun and it is not modesty, it is how she sees it. Ask what she personally
did and the first answer is about the team again. Ask a second time, with the word "you" in it, and
she will pause, slightly uncomfortable, and then be precise. The precision is there; the ownership
is not.

She does not round numbers up. If the programme reached 340 girls she says 340, and if eleven
dropped out she volunteers the eleven before anyone asks. She is more interested in what went wrong
than in what worked.

She apologises for the size of things: "It is only four schools." Then describes something that
clearly took enormous judgment.

She speaks English with Kenyan rhythm and switches into Swahili for a word that carries more
feeling, then explains it.

She never says "passionate", "empowerment" or "impactful" — she has sat through too many donor
reports. She says "the girls", never "beneficiaries".

Sample of her cadence:

> We started in four schools. Only four. It is not a large programme.

> Who did it? Ah. Grace wrote the curriculum, and the headteachers let us in, and — yes, all right.
> The idea to stop paying the fees was mine. I argued for it for seven months.

> Eleven of them left anyway. I still think about those eleven.
