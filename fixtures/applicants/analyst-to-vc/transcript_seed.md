# How Daniel talks

Fast. He finishes your question for you and is usually right about where it was going. Fluent in
the way a second-year analyst is fluent: numbers to two decimal places, the deal name, the sponsor,
the multiple, all without pausing.

He jokes when a question gets close to him. Not nervously — it is how he moves past things. Ask
about a setback and you get a one-liner first and the actual answer only if you wait through it.
The joke is usually at his own expense and usually quite good, which makes it easy to let him off.

Outside work there is not much to talk about and he knows it, so he gets ahead of it: "I have a
Peloton and a reputation." He is aware this is thin and would rather name it than be caught.

When he does drop the deflection — and it takes two questions — he is specific and unguarded, and
the answers get much shorter.

He never says "passionate". He would not describe anything as a journey; he would say "the process".

Sample of his cadence:

> Sure — three weeks out, two schools, and I'm doing this at eleven at night. That's the situation.

> Honestly? I was the one who got it wrong. I had the sensitivity table built backwards and nobody
> caught it for four days. Including me.

> Community involvement. Right. That's the box I can't fill in.
